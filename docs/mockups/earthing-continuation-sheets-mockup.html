Earthing schematic — wrap width and continuation sheets

Proposal, 2026-11-10 · follows v2.081 (bands in columns, PR 501) · Earthing schematic E-EAR-… · black-and-white output, as today

Where v2.081 leaves it. The bands now flow into columns, and the pit schedule gets its own column. A nine-substation site prints about twice as large as before. Two things remain:

  • Blank space above and below. A wide substation band (up to about 2,900 px before it wraps) makes every column wide. Two band columns plus the schedule then fill the sheet's width before its height, and three would be too wide.
  • Text still small on big sites. On the nine-substation example, 10 px labels print at about 1.0 mm on A1 and 0.45 mm on A3. The app's own readability limit for drawings (0.2 mm per px, so 2 mm for a 10 px label) is not met, and nothing tells the user.

v2.081 today — two band columns, blank space above and below

Nine-substation example: 12 band rows on A1. Labels print at about 1.0 mm. The columns are as wide as the widest band, so the drawing runs out of width with height to spare.

Step 1 — the band wrap width is chosen with the layout (one sheet)

Today a substation wraps onto a second row only past about 2,900 px. Instead, the app tries a few wrap widths (2,900, 2,300, 1,800 and 1,400 px). For each, it lays out the columns and the schedule as in v2.081, and keeps whichever prints largest. A wide substation then becomes two narrower rows, each marked 1/2 and 2/2 in its zone, with its links shown as today's "to EP-…" stubs. That allows three band columns where only two fitted before. Each row still holds whole equipment, never cut through a symbol.

Same content: the four wide substations wrap to two rows each, so there are 16 narrower rows in three columns. I measured this with the v2.081 code on the nine-substation example at A1. Wrapping at 1,400 px instead of 2,900 px raises the scale from 0.104 to 0.142 mm per px (labels about 1.0 → 1.4 mm, +37%). The large single site goes from 0.171 to 0.193 (+13%). The best width differs per project (1,800 px was worse than 2,300 px on the nine-substation site), which is why the app tries several. There is still one sheet, and labels on big sites are still under 2 mm.

Also considered: putting narrow bands side by side on one line. Once the wrap width is chosen this way, the rows are close in width and it gains little, so it is left out.

Step 2 — continuation sheets when text would print under the minimum

If the best layout from Step 1 would print labels smaller than a minimum (proposed 2.0 mm, the app's existing 0.2 mm per px limit), the schematic continues onto more sheets. Each sheet holds as many whole lines as fit at the minimum size. The pit schedule goes on the last sheet, or on its own sheet if it does not fit there. A link to a pit on another sheet is drawn as today's row-crossing stub, with the sheet number added: to EP-TX(N)-04 (sheet 2). Small sites stay on one sheet. This works like the earthing riser sheets today: each continuation sheet is a part of the earthing sheet in the drawing set, numbered with the set and listed in the drawing register.

Nine-substation example at a 2.0 mm minimum (0.2 mm per px): an A1 drawing area holds about 3,600 × 2,600 px, so about 4 band rows per sheet. That gives three or four A1 sheets, with the pit schedule on the last one. A 2,900 px band spans about 80% of the sheet width. Labels print at 2.0 mm or larger.

The paper size and band width have to agree

At the 2 mm minimum, an A3 drawing area holds only about 1,500 × 1,150 px, which is one band row. Step 1's wrap widths would then be limited to what the paper holds (1,400 px on A3), so even a wide substation reaches the minimum. That costs one sheet per row.

Decisions needed

  1. Step 1 only, Step 2 only, or both? (Step 1 is a layout change on one sheet, measured at +37% / +13%; Step 2 adds sheets to the set.)
  2. Minimum printed label height: 2.0 mm (the app's existing drawing limit) or 2.5 mm?
  3. Auto paper for a big earthing schematic: go up to A1 first and add sheets only then (proposed), or stay on A3 and add sheets?
  4. Continuation sheet numbers: separate numbers E-EAR-001, -002, -003 (proposed, as the earthing riser parts), or one number with "sheet 1 of 3"?
  5. Pit schedule on a big site: on the last drawing sheet when it fits (proposed), or always on a sheet of its own?
