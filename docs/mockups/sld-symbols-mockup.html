SLD symbols: today and proposed

2026-11-16 · proposal, nothing built yet · "Today" is drawn by the app's own System diagram code (v2.161, printed black), one board with every earth leakage case. "Proposed" uses the same text classes and positions with the redrawn symbols.

1 · Today, DEWA style: feeders with earth leakage protection

  • F02 ELCB 30 mA and F03 RCBO: the dashed trip link runs into "10 kA · 30mA ELCB"; the toroid hangs below the breaker, on the cable just above the panel frame line; the text says MCB and ELCB (two devices) under one RCBO symbol.
  • F04 MCB + RCCB: the RCCB is a word in a box, joined to the power line by a solid line, so it reads as a branch circuit; its toroid floats on the frame line; the box runs into F03's text.
  • F05 isolator + RCD: drawn as an RCCB but labelled "40A TPN MCB". F06 MCCB + ELR: a word in a box on a solid line; nothing shows the relay tripping the breaker (the library's own ELR symbol is not used).
  • F07 DOL + UVR: the U< box touches the breaker blade and the DOL box crosses the panel frame line. F08, F09: the kWh boxes cover the cable text; the local isolator is a hand-drawn slanted line and dot.

Today, standard style: the same feeders

Same symbols: the trip links touch "20 A · 30 mA", and the RCCB and ELR boxes and loose toroids are as above.

2 · Proposed symbols

RCBO (breaker with earth leakage)
Toroid inside the device; trip link on the blade side, away from the text.
RCCB / ELCB
Switch-disconnector contact (bar and circle), as its label says; same toroid.
Circuit breaker (MCB / MCCB)
Same symbol; the blade pivots higher so all breakers share the foot that carries a toroid.
Non-automatic breaker (NA)
It cannot trip, so no × : drawn as a switch-disconnector, text "MCCB (NA)".
ACB, withdrawable
Double draw-out chevrons at both terminals, readable at drawing size.
Earth leakage relay with CBCT
Toroid on the cable, relay box on the left, dashed trip link up to the breaker.
Undervoltage release (UVR)
Acts on the breaker (dashed link), not wired to the power line.
Bus coupler
A breaker drawn across the busbar, N/O or N/C below; today a box with a slash.
Local isolator at the equipment
The library switch-disconnector, not a hand-drawn slanted line and dot.
RCCB after an MCB
Two devices in series, each with its own rating line; today a word in a box on a solid line.

3 · Proposed, on the feeders (DEWA style)

Switching devices drawn 1.5 times larger, in a 24-unit slot that starts 8 units higher; every text row stays where it is. The toroid sits inside the device, under the blade, and its trip link stays on the blade side. The text names the device that is drawn: RCBO, RCCB, MCCB. ELR: CBCT on the cable, relay on the left, dashed trip link to the breaker. UVR: a release acting on the breaker. Meters at the load end, left of the line; the local isolator is the library switch-disconnector.

3b · ELCB drawn as in your sketch: core-balance CT and ELCB box

The core-balance CT on the cable (a ring core with one turn of winding wrapped round it) wired to the ELCB box, and the sensitivity above the box: one row of its own under the breaker's text, as in your sketch. The breaker's text no longer carries the mA, so text and drawing agree (MCCB, then ELCB). A board with any ELCB gets one more row (24 units; 32 in the standard style), so all its columns stay aligned. ELR and RCCB written in the remarks use the same box with their own word; RCBO keeps the breaker symbol with its toroid (section 2).

4 · Kept as they are

These 41 of the 55 library symbols keep their shape (the RMU in open PR 617 too). The other switching symbols (isolator, contactor, fuse switch, shunt trip, changeover) keep their shapes on the new breaker foot.
